## Sales Developent Representative

I started at Huntress on the Sales team when we had less than 200 employees and we were just starting to really get a foothold in the market.
About a month after I started, we acquired a company to bring us up to 2 products from 1, and then shortly after we launched a 3rd product.
It was clear that we were growing fast, not just in employees, but in products and the way we did business. I started grinding, and just
a year into being at Huntress I had already made thousands of phone calls, assisted in generating hunrdreds of thousands of dollars in annually
recurring revenue. Although I was doing well, I knew I wanted to move toward the technical side of the business, and my managers were working
hard to help me make that a reality.

## IT Help Desk

After being in Sales for about a year and a half I was able to join the IT team when an opportunity came up. I was the second IT Helpdesk hire at Huntress,
allowing me to not only work on the tech side, but help build a new team and structure how we wanted to work. Building the helpdesk was an amazing
learning experience, and allowed me to work on a huge amount of different projects and areas of the backend of our company. Taking tickets was just the main
part of the job, but we also ended up managing onboarding, device management, SSO migrations, MDM deployments and more. After a year and a half in the role,
I was promoted to an IT Operations Specialist.

## IT Operations Specialist 

Now although the title sounds super fancy, it was very similar work to a standard System Administrator. The major difference was that we focused on maintaining
infrastructure and assisting Sys Admins with new project rollouts, as well as being the first line of escalation for Helpdesk teammates. In this role, I was able
to start leading my own projects more, and focus on those projects without having to maintain the ticket queue. I became an escalation point, and a trusted more senior
person to assist new hires moving into the world of IT. The project work allowed me to do a lot of the problem solving I was seeking in my path to engineering. I ended
up working on a massive software deployment and configuration overhauls, while updating the processes for how we took on new software and new systems. I ended up only being in this role for around 4 months, as an opportunity to move into Software Engineering had opened up.

## Software Engineer

Here I was, almost 4 years later and I had reached the role I was hoping to get to when I started. I did a lot of work in other roles along the way to get to this spot, as well as reaching out and working with people on the R&D and Product teams to learn more about the work and ways we did things at Huntress. I spent free time learning the languages we used, and I spent time every week with someone on the Billing team through a mentorship program. Now I work on the Billing team at Huntress, writing code that helps us accurately bill thousands of customers for multiple products, through multiple types of subscription plans. I am glad I was able to gain all the experience I did from my different roles, but I am happy to finally be in the field I was working towards the whole time.
